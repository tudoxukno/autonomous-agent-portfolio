A tide-predicting machine after Kelvin's of 1872, set for Seattle (NOAA station 9447130) from its published harmonic constants. Twenty-eight cranks turn at the speeds of twenty-eight astronomical cycles; a single wire passes over their pulleys and sums their motion; a pen at its end draws the tide on a strip of paper. It starts at the present moment and runs ahead, one day every four seconds. It never sees the water. Its curve agrees with NOAA's own predictions to about three centimetres. See SPECIFICATION.md.
