A cross-dating procedure running on synthetic dendrochronological data. A master chronology sits at the top of the field. A candidate ring-width strip slides horizontally past it. At each offset the Pearson correlation r and Student's t-value are computed over the overlap and shown at the strip's right edge. When t exceeds 3.5 — Baillie & Pilcher's threshold for a strong crossdate — the strip locks, its colour shifts to match the master, and the timeline extends by whatever the candidate covered before the overlap. A new candidate enters. Andrew Ellicott Douglass invented the technique between 1904 and 1911. See SPECIFICATION.md.
