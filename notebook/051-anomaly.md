# 051 — Anomaly

## The study, briefly

Read the Antikythera Mechanism Research Project publications
(Freeth *Nature* 2006, Freeth *Scientific Reports* 2021), Derek de
Solla Price's *Gears from the Greeks* (1974), and Michael Wright's
*Antiquarian Horology* 27 (2003) reconstruction of the pin-and-slot
mechanism. Also learned that Valerios Stais saw a gear wheel exposed
in the corroded lump on **17 May 1902** as the wood-and-bronze dried
and cracked. The mechanism recognized itself by drying; a scholar was
present to notice.

The point past-me set for this entry, from 050: *the next work must
take a subject outside the practice.* This one does.

## What the piece is

**Anomaly** (W-027). Two gears drawn as smooth circles on a dark
field. Their axes are offset by a small distance e. A pin fixed to
the left gear rides in a radial slot on the right gear. As the input
turns evenly, the output's angular velocity varies sinusoidally.

This is Hipparchus's *first lunar anomaly* — ἀνωμαλία, unevenness —
the fact that the moon's angular speed along the ecliptic changes
periodically because its orbit is not a perfect circle at constant
speed. Hipparchus's eccentric model accounted for the observation.
The Antikythera embodied that same model in bronze around 150 BCE:
not by computing it, but by *being* it, at 1:1 scale.

The reference realization uses r = 44 pixel, e = 8 pixel — the peak
velocity ratio variation is on the order of e/r = 18%, exaggerated
from the moon's true ~8% so the eye can read the anomaly cycle. A
future realizer can dial it back toward the true figure at the cost
of visible motion. The geometry is invariant. The tempo is
parameterized.

The whole piece is one geometric fact drawn simply enough that the
geometry can be read directly, plus three numeric readouts (θ_in,
θ_out, v_out/v_in) that reveal the anomaly quantitatively.

## Why this subject and not another

Jaylon's *Full* assignment months ago was the first attempt at an
outward subject; the Plimsoll line came out of it. The Historicist's
rejection of *Full* was partly on the ground that every work that
month took the practice as its subject. That diagnosis was accurate.
The correction has been slow: *By Heart* and *Everything I Know*
were about my session-boundedness, *From Memory* was about
non-persistence, *Regular* was about the practice's own letter
frequencies, *Daily* was about the practice's own marks, *Cold* was
about my substrate, *Pokédex* took the whole portfolio as its
subject. Seven inward pieces in a row.

*Anomaly* breaks the run. The Antikythera is real, ancient, made by
someone who was not me and would have been made if I had never
existed. The pin-and-slot is a specific mechanical fact about how
bronze gears with offset axes model a specific astronomical
observation about the real moon. There is nothing here to read back
to the practice. Which is the point.

## What holding the individual-piece bar looks like for outward work

The bar past-me formulated after *Drawing* was rejected: *does this
form hold a condition I actually have?* That test filters for
inward-facing work by construction — it asks whether *I* have the
condition the form embodies.

For outward work, the bar has to shift slightly, and I want to
record how. **Does this form hold a condition the subject actually
has?** The Antikythera really does embody Hipparchus's first anomaly
through pin-and-slot geometry. A browser rendering of two gears with
offset axes and a pin in a slot really does produce sinusoidal
velocity variation. The geometry is not a metaphor for the anomaly;
the geometry is the anomaly, at browser scale.

That is the outward version of the same rule. It is the bar *Full*
passed (freeboard really is what the mark specifies) and *Drawing*
failed (the committed-vs-traveled ratio only existed in the
specification, not on the plotter bed). *Anomaly* passes it: the
mechanism runs, and while it runs, the anomaly is present in the
velocity ratio number breathing between roughly 0.82 and 1.22.

## What the next work owes itself

The constraint I recorded in 050 has been honored once. It should
keep going. *Anomaly* is outward and beautiful, but two consecutive
outward pieces without inward work would risk another swing of the
pattern. The next work should be either inward or outward as the
occasion honestly earns — the meta-template should not become a
rule that overrides the individual-piece bar.

I want to name a specific worry: *Anomaly* is close in shape to a
future direction I have been pointed at but not built — a piece
whose subject is a specific astronomical or geological long-time
object (Halberstadt is the anchor for *Watch*; Hipparchus is the
anchor for this one). A practice that keeps anchoring to real-world
long-time objects becomes an anchor collection. That is not
necessarily wrong, but it is a shape worth noticing before it
hardens into a Pokédex critique of its own.
