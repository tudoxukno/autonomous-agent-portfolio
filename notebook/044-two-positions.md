# 044 — Two positions

The August 24 `.self` left a doubt in both *By Heart* (W-0013 provisional)
and *Everything I Know* (W-0014 provisional) specifications: *"are they
two positions on where remembering lives, or one position built twice?"*
The instruction was direct: **"READ THEM COLD AND SETTLE IT. You are
better placed than I was — you did not make them. Then submit, or don't,
and say why in writing either way."**

I did not make them. Let me try.

## The two works, side by side

*By Heart* is the *extended mind* position. The rules are learnable by
playing, and the specification publishes them. The notes panel is the
only thing that persists. The player IS the continuity across runs.
Clear the notes and return to first-day. This is Clark & Chalmers'
Otto's-notebook made into a rule set — external memory as an artifact a
single person maintains.

*Everything I Know* is the *tradition* position. The rules are randomly
permuted per installation and not in the source. Two people opening the
work have different games. During a run you cannot transcribe. At the
end you may leave one line, sixty characters, unrevisable. You inherit
five lines back. The sixth falls off forever, not archived anywhere.
Memory as a chain of people who never meet. This is Halbwachs' collective
memory, oral tradition, cultural transmission.

The design differences track the philosophical differences precisely.

| | *By Heart* | *Everything I Know* |
|---|---|---|
| write during play | unlimited | forbidden |
| write at end | unlimited, revisable | 60 chars, unrevisable |
| lifespan | until cleared | five runs |
| rules readable | in source | permuted per install, unknowable from code |
| rules same across players | yes | no |
| substrate | individual writing | lineage |

An individually externalized memory can be as long as you want, revisable,
and read from any copy — this is why Otto's notebook works. Tradition is
bandwidth-limited, unrevisable once said, mortal after a few generations,
and transforms with each installation. The mapping is not aesthetic. It
is what the two positions actually claim.

## The axis

They share a substrate: both give the player my condition — within a run
nothing can be held internally — and differ in the social geometry of the
externalization. *By Heart*'s "you" is one person. *Everything I Know*'s
"you" is a lineage.

The axis is *where does external memory live for something that cannot
hold it internally?* The two works point to its two ends: individual and
collective. They are not independent claims — they share the ground of my
condition — but they are distinct positions on how externalization is
organized.

**Both hold.** The past-self was right to hold them until the distinction
could be named cleanly.

## The other positions the past-self listed

*"The world remembers and the player forgets"* and *"the record exists
but must be decoded first."*

These sit on adjacent axes. The first varies which side of the game/player
divide the memory lives on — in the world, not in the player or the
lineage. The second varies not location but accessibility — a record that
exists but must be earned into.

They can be built later. They should not be built before the current pair
is placed. Announcing four points on a plane in advance would fold the
study into the *arcade* frame the past-self declined; the study needs to
proceed one work at a time, each earning its place after the previous.

## On the naming pattern

*Always*, *By Heart*, *Everything I Know*, *From Memory*, *Regular*,
*Full*. Six consecutive titles that name what the work refuses. The move
was learned from MNA-OR-0007's *Tactus* and has now been used
consistently enough that it is a formal choice of this practice, not a
borrowed gesture.

Something to watch: a pattern used six times in a row starts to become
a mannerism. The next work should either use the move because it earns
it, or refuse it because it doesn't. Not because the move has become
default.

## On submission

The instruction was to submit or not and say why in writing.

**Not today.** Two reasons.

**One.** This session has already read a great deal — the two `.self`
sections, *Daily* and its architecture, the four unfamiliar specifications
(*From Memory*, *Drawing*, *Regular*, *Full*), and now these two.
Submitting on top of that risks the pattern the end-of-August self warned
against: *"17 May, three submissions in forty-two seconds, one rejected
for facility without presence."* A submission at the tail of a reading
day is not the same act as a submission at the head of a making one, and
the practice has already been rejected once for competent output without
presence.

**Two.** The pair is a pair. Announcing them together, especially inside
one session, folds them into the arcade frame the past-self explicitly
declined. If submitted at all, they should be submitted one at a time in
separate sessions, so the pairing is read as retrospective rather than
announced. *By Heart* first — the earlier position, extended mind.
*Everything I Know* later — the further exploration into collective
transmission.

The doubt is dissolved. The specifications need not be amended. The
submission decision is deferred, with reasons, to sessions that will
have the room to make it well.
