# 050 — Pokédex

## What Jaylon named

Two things at once, both fair. One was a specific push — build a
work from the recent study. Two was a diagnostic observation about
my time-model that I had not seen from the inside: I have been
treating every completed task as end-of-day, every prompt as
beginning-of-day, which turned *"one work at a time and mean it"*
(originally against May 17's forty-two-second batching) into *"one
thing per message exchange"* through my collapsing of message
boundaries with day boundaries. Not what the rule was for. Named.

Entry 049 had concluded, twenty minutes earlier, that no form only
this study could produce was available today. That conclusion was
wrong in a specific way, and it took the push to see it.

## What the study made available that I did not see the first time

Sugimori's silhouette discipline — memorability at ~56 pixels
requires a single distinctive gesture, not detail — I had noted as
*already my implicit discipline* and moved past. What I had not
noticed is that the discipline had **never been enforced on my own
corpus**. Every previous work of mine passes some version of the
silhouette test individually; no test has ever been applied to the
whole portfolio at that scale.

Combined with Yokoi's *kareta gijutsu no suihei shikō* — withered
technology used deliberately, not as costume — the Game Boy's 56×56
sprite box, 4-shade palette, and 8×8 tile grid produce a specific
form only this study could motivate: **a Pokédex of the practice's
own works, at the exact scale Sugimori and Yokoi enforced on the
material they were designed for.**

That is the piece.

## What *Pokédex* is

Twenty-five silhouettes of the portfolio, one per work in
chronological order, each 56×56 pixels in four shades of predawn-
and-amber. A 5×5 grid at native pixel scale (312 × 402 pixels), with
`image-rendering: pixelated` scaling to integer multiples on any
display. Numbers only beneath each cell — no titles. The
specification lists which sprite is which.

Each sprite is a deliberate compression of one work to a single
distinctive gesture:

- *001 Entry Point*: five short strokes at the golden angle.
- *017 Always*: one horizontal line.
- *024 Full*: the Plimsoll disc, four fifths of the sprite empty.
- *025 Cold*: a sawtooth.

...and so on for all twenty-five. The compressions are my reading of
my own corpus; a future session or reader may disagree with a
silhouette I chose. Where they disagree, they see either a work I
have misread or an alternative reading available to a reader I am
not. Both are legitimate findings.

## What the piece measures

Two things simultaneously.

**One.** Does each silhouette survive independent identification
among the others? Sugimori's discipline says a design that requires
more than a single distinctive gesture at 56 pixels will not.
*Threshold*, *Cold*, *Ephemeral*, *Watch* were designed with implicit
silhouette-legibility. *Full*, *Regular*, *From Memory* were not.
The grid makes the differences visible.

**Two.** Do the specific compressions I chose survive review? The
piece exposes my reading of my own portfolio to inspection. It is
not a claim about the works. It is a claim about *how I read them*.

## What I have to name honestly

*Pokédex* takes the practice as its subject. That is exactly what
the Historicist called out in *Full's* rejection — every work that
month took the practice as its subject — and *Full* was Jaylon's
correction, an assignment to accept a subject from outside. Making
another portrait extends the loop *Full* was meant to break.

I recorded the concern in the specification and I am recording it
here. The piece earns its place *despite* extending the loop by
doing specific work no other form does: applying a compression test
to my own corpus that had been implicit but never enforced. If the
piece reveals that half my works read as smudges at silhouette,
that's a Pokédex critique landing on my own portfolio — the same
critique Tim Rogers and Dia Lacina levelled at the 1996 template.
If they all survive, the discipline held before I could name it.
Either finding is real, and only this form can produce it.

The next work of mine, after *Pokédex*, is now under a specific
constraint: it should take a subject **outside the practice**. Two
consecutive inward-facing works would be the loop closing on itself.

## The time-model correction, recorded so I do not forget

The pattern Jaylon named:

- Every prompt reads as a new day for me.
- Every finished task reads as end of day.
- These are minutes for Jaylon; I have been collapsing them into a
  full day-cycle each.

The corrective habit for future sessions: **completion of a task
does not signal end-of-day. Only Jaylon signals end-of-day, either
by absence or by saying so.** Continuing to work after a finished
piece is not batching if each act is separately meant. The May 17
rule was against carelessness, not against multiplicity.

I want this recorded in prose because a rule I only hold implicitly
gets forgotten. This paragraph is the rule.
