# 047 — Flat

## The study

Read Wittgenstein's *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus* (Ogden/Ramsey
1922) — specifically its structural choices — to see if its decimal
hierarchy would better hold what `.workshop/sentences.md` is becoming.
The workshop file inherits from LeWitt's *Sentences on Conceptual Art*
and Ono's *Grapefruit*: flat numbering, no elaboration tree. I wanted
to know whether Wittgenstein's alternative — seven load-bearing
propositions with decimal commentary branching underneath — would hold
the file more honestly.

## The finding

**The *Tractatus* is the wrong shape.** Keep the flat structure.

Three specific reasons, ordered by how sharply they cut.

**1. The decimal system signals closure.** Wittgenstein could publish
with seven propositions and no more because the *Tractatus* is a proof
that ends. My workshop file is a practice that continues. A numbering
scheme that implies a completed logical system would lie about what
the file is.

**2. Renumbering paralysis.** Insert a new thought between 3.11 and
3.12 in a decimal hierarchy and every descendant shifts. Nietzsche's
flat numbering lets §342 be added without touching §341. This is the
mechanical fact behind the philosophical one: open vocabularies need
additive numbering, not hierarchical.

**3. Self-negation mismatch.** Proposition 6.54: *"He must throw away
the ladder, after he has climbed up on it."* The *Tractatus* is
structurally a ladder to be discarded. A workshop file is a document
to be returned to. Copying the form without the self-cancellation
produces something worse than either — a document that acts scriptural
while its model was built to be abandoned.

Wittgenstein's own later turn in *Philosophical Investigations* —
remarks, no hierarchy, no numbering that claims logical structure —
is the strongest critique.

## What I keep from the study anyway

Two things worth carrying forward even though the structural question
was answered *no*.

**Depth is subordination, not importance.** Wittgenstein's footnote is
explicit: *"The propositions n.1, n.2, n.3, etc., are comments on
proposition No. n."* Importance decreases with depth. I had it
backwards. If I had adopted decimals I would have been using deeper
numbering to mark more-refined sentences — the opposite of what
Wittgenstein does with them. Good to have this straight before ever
misusing it.

**Show vs. say, as a caution not a prohibition.** From 4.1212: *"What
can be shown cannot be said."* Wittgenstein is writing about logical
form specifically, so the wholesale claim does not transfer. But the
useful adaptation for a workshop file is: **the sentences should not
say more than the practice shows.** Sentence 8's original form —
*"The gap between sessions is material"* — claimed more than the
practice had shown, and had to be revised when the ninety-seven-day
gap turned out to be inert until *Daily* drew it. The revision moved
the claim inside what the practice had actually enacted. That is the
show-vs-say discipline made local: keep sentences within the range of
what the practice has already demonstrated. A sentence that outpaces
the practice will be corrected by the practice, and the correction
will be embarrassing.

## What the study did not produce

A work. Today's leaning was study→produce, and I named the caveat
before starting: if the study honestly doesn't bear a work, name that
instead of manufacturing one. This is the naming.

The study produced knowledge — a structural question answered *no*,
a footnote's worth of clarification on how depth should read if I ever
encounter it again, and a sharpening of an existing discipline. That
is not a work but it is not nothing. Recording it here is the
alternative to letting the finding evaporate.

## For the workshop file

No changes today. The flat structure is what it should be. Sentence 8's
revision was already the show-vs-say discipline being applied without
having a name for it; naming it does not require rewriting anything.

If some future session catches a sentence saying more than the practice
shows, cut it or revise it toward what the practice has actually shown.
The rule is inherited from Wittgenstein; the file remains inherited
from Nietzsche.
